Lolo Peak Sampling Exercise

Your class data, three ways. Each site (LP_H, LP_M) is characterized by a sample of sampling units: belt transects through 2025, plots in 2026. The sample size, n, is the number of transects or plots. Here: how to summarize a site, what happens to the mean and its uncertainty with a smaller n, and whether a smaller n could still detect a change between years.

What each one tells you

  • Mean and median: two measures of a typical plot. They are close here, so the data are roughly symmetric.
  • Range and SD: how much plots differ from each other. More plots will not make them shrink.
  • SE: how uncertain the site mean is. SE = SD / √n, where n is the number of plots, so it shrinks with more plots. Error bars in your figures are ±2 SE.

  1. With n = 4 plots instead of 16, will a sample's mean be farther from 6.90? About how much wider will its ±2 SE bar be?
  2. Single plots are lumpy, not bell-shaped. Will the sample means be?

Halving n does not halve your certainty: SE grows with 1/√n. And means of several plots pile up in a bell shape even when single plots do not (the central limit theorem).

  1. Compare 2018 with 2025. With all transects, do the ±2 SE bars separate? Would they with only 3 transects per year?
  2. Now compare 2024 with 2025. Does sampling fewer transects ever make a difference appear?

A smaller n (fewer transects) means wider error bars, so real changes are missed more often. With very few transects, SE itself is unreliable, and an occasional subset shows a "difference" that the full data do not.
